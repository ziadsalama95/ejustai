## Personal Info

Dr.-Ing. Mohamed G. A. Nassef is an Associate Professor at the School of Innovative Design and Engineering, Egypt-Japan University of Science and Technology (E-JUST). He earned his B.Sc. in Production Engineering from Alexandria University (Egypt) in 2005. In 2009, he was awarded a DAAD (German Academic Exchange Service) full scholarship to pursue his Ph.D. at Bremen University in Germany, where he conducted research on dynamic optimization of machine tool feed drive systems until he received his doctorate in 2013. Subsequently, he was awarded a Fulbright Postdoctoral Fellowship (2016), further strengthening his international research profile.

Dr. Nassef leads an active research group focused on two interlinked tracks: fault diagnosis of rotating machinery and tribological studies of nano- and green-lubricants. His specific research interests include early fault detection and diagnosis of rotating machinery, advanced signal processing including Morlet wavelet filteration and variational mode decomposition, electric signature analysis, oil analysis, vibrations analysis, heuristic optimization algorithms, and machine learning algorithms. The other track is the studies on the development and tribological assessment of novel, bio-derived nano-lubricants for sustainable rotating machinery including cutting fluids, lubricant oils, and grease.

Beyond research, Dr. Nassef has extensive teaching experience across a broad spectrum of undergraduate and postgraduate courses in mechanical engineering, machine design, and tribology. He actively supervises graduate research, mentors students, and contributes to curriculum development, academic planning, and university-level coordination. He regularly provides technical consultancy to industrial partners on fault diagnostics of rotating machinery.

As a DAAD research ambassador and alumnus, Dr. Nassef maintains strong international research collaborations, particularly with German research institutes. He has led joint research activities and is highly experienced in multicultural academic environments, having collaborated with researchers and students from diverse African backgrounds. Through his role at E-JUST and its strategic partnerships with Japanese universities, he has developed deep engagement with Japanese educational practices and culture.
