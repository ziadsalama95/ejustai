Egypt entry visa:
How to Apply for a Visa to Enter Egypt:
Residence permit:
According to Egyptian regulations, international students get residence permits for one 
year, renewed annually. RIA oﬀice supports international students to get/renew their 
residence permits in Egypt through:
 
05
•  You may acquire a visa application form from any Egyptian consulate/embassy.
•  Do not wait until the last minute to apply. Allow yourself several weeks for getting the 
visa as usually visa processing takes several weeks.
•  After arrival to Egypt, you must apply for the residency permit at the Egyptian passport 
administration with help of E-JUST RIA oﬀice.
•  Some nationalities, must acquire passport registration stamp from the passport 
administration within the first ten days of arrival.
•  Issuing all the supporting documents which are needed to apply for the residence permit.
• Accompanying students to the passport administration to proceed with his/her 
application for residence permit.
•  The student must apply for the residence permit after his/her arrival  in Egypt (withinthe 
first two weeks of arrival)
•  Renewing the residence permit, usually takes a while. So the student should start the 
application for renewing the residency one month before the expiration date.  
•   Egypt requires that you have a student visa in order to enter the country. You will need 
to check with the Egyptian consulate/embassy in your country.
•   As part of the application process, you must submit a copy of your passport, along with 
the acceptance letter provided by E-JUST Regional and International Aﬀairs oﬀice (RIA). 
Additional documents like medical certificate and bank statements might also be 
required.
•  Kindly note that issuance and renewal of student visas is at the discretion of the 
Egyptian government.

Finances & Budgeting
Financial arrangements before you leave:
Cost of living usually varies according to individual studentʼs choices and lifestyle. 
We advise you students to have some cash money (In Dollars) when you come to Egypt. 
Upon arrival at Cairo international airport, exchange into Egyptian pounds what you
need for your first month in Egypt.
Egypt is a cash-based society. It is necessary to always have a minimum of LE 300 in the 
pocket. In addition, new students must be certain to bring enough start-up cash, since it 
may take up to one month to open a bank account or to receive your monthly stipend (in 
case you have a scholarship). E-JUST does not provide an up- front financial aid.
Keep your bank account open at home, or open up a bank account before you leave. Be 
sure to check with your bank to determine what fees you may be charged for each overseas 
ATM transaction.
Students who are awarded a scholarship will receive their stipend on a monthly basis (by 
the end of each month).

The oﬀicial currency of Egypt is the Egyptian Pound (EGP). The symbol used for the EGP is L.E 
or in Arabic ( .)ج.مIn Arabic, Egyptian pounds are called “gineih” and piasters are called “qirsh”
The Egyptian pound is mainly a paper currency and there are various denominations. 
Paper notes are available in the form of 5 ,10 ,20 ,50 ,100 ,200 and 1. Coins are available in

Coping with the culture
The choice to live and study abroad is a major undertaking in cross-cultural living and 
learning. It will not be easy, and the process will progress over a number of months. The 
payoﬀ, if successful, is immense. You will learn about yourself and your home culture as 
you learn about Egyptians and their culture. It is common for new arrivals to go through a 
period of culture shock, a (sometimes) anguishing period of reaction and adjustment to a 
new, unfamiliar environment.
Dress Code
Egypt is known to be a Muslim conservative country but unlike the norm in other Arabian 
countries, we do not have a traditional dress. However, we advise our students to dress 
modestly  to avoid any unwanted attention. For example, no crop tops and no miniskirts 
and short dresses. But in touristic places, you can dress freely.  
Being a student at E-JUST requires you to be responsible of your actions, abide by the rules 
and be as presentable as possible. E-JUST has its own dress code for all of its students, staﬀ 
and academics to follow in order to maintain the universityʼs public image and professionalism, 
thus, as long as you are on campus and common dorm areas, kindly abide by the dress code. 
However, you are free to wear what you wish outside of campus.

Food
The Egyptian cuisine is known to be rich in spices and herbs such as; 
cumin, garlic, coriander, mint, cloves etc. It shares some qualities with 
the Eastern Mediterranean cuisine. 
The Egyptian cuisine makes heavy use of legumes, vegetables and fruit. 
The most common meats in Egyptian cuisine are; pigeon, chicken and 
beef but they are more on the expensive side. 
The food in Alexandria and the coast of Egypt tend to use a great deal of 
fish and seafood, Our traditional drink is red tea “Shai”.

Religion:
Religion in Egypt controls many aspects of social life and is endorsed by law, and the state 
religion of Egypt is Islam. 
The two most recognized religions in Egypt are Islam and Christianity. Approximately 
%94.9 of the population of Egypt is Muslim and the Christians represent about 5% of the 
population. The vast majority, %95, of Christians in Egypt belong to the Coptic Orthodox 
Church of Alexandria. Freedom of worship and beliefs are recognized in Egypt. Mosques 
and churches are to be found everywhere.
Language: 
 The oﬀicial language of Egypt is standard Arabic, however, the day-to-day language used 
is Egyptian Arabic, which is a local dialect. 
On campus, most E-JUST students, staﬀ and academics are able to speak English. 
Our international students are able to pick up the local dialect easily and with the help of 
Arabic courses in the preparatory year, speaking in Egyptian Arabic wonʼt be a challenge.
Customs and Traditions:
In the Egyptian society, respecting the privacy and the existence of the opposite sex is a 
highly important aspect in the day to day life. 
For example you will find segregated facilities like separate metro cars for men and 
women. Also at E-JUST, men are not allowed into the womenʼs dormitories and vice versa. 
Same goes for separate training hours for both at the activities hub to respect their 
freedom and privacy. From that light, Showing public aﬀection is considered against the 
societyʼs & E-JUSTʼs moral codes.
We understand that E-JUST international students come from diﬀerent cultures and 
traditions and it may be challenging to adapt to the Egyptian culture, however, it is 
essential for foreigners to understand and respect the culture of the country they are in.

Weather:
Egypt is known for its warm desert-like climate but unexpectedly temperatures can vary 
considerably throughout the year. For example, Alexandria and Borg-Al Arab can get rainier 
than Cairo and it definitely diﬀers from one location to the other.
 
Average Temperatures in Egypt: 
 •  Summer in Egypt varies from 33° Celsius/93° Fahrenheit to well over 43° Celsius/110° Fahrenheit
•  Fall in Egypt varies from 18 ° Celsius/63° Fahrenheit to around 28° Celsius/83° Fahrenheit
•  Winter in Egypt varies from 9° Celsius/48° Fahrenheit to about 18° Celsius/64° Fahrenheit
•  Spring in Egypt varies from 15° Celsius/93° Fahrenheit to 30° Celsius/86° Fahrenheit

Getting around Alexandria and Cairo:
Metro: The metro system or the subway metro  in Cairo covers many areas. 
It has 3 lines and it covers 92  stations all over the city. It costs around 5 to 10 LE.
Railways: The Egyptian railway system is the best way for long journeys. 
There are three classes of carriage on the train; the first and the second ones are 
air-conditioned.
There are sleeping trains but the price will be much more than normal ones.  It costs 
around 100 to 250 LE one way trip.
El Tram: it is more like metro but much slower and not underground. 
It is very cheap but reaches far and many places in Alexandria. It costs around 5 LE.
Buses: Buses are one of the most popular means of transportation in Egypt. 
They are wide spread. It is considered one of the cheapest transportation means  and it is 
easily accessed. 
When you pay the fare, you should take a ticket to prove your payment later. 
 Each bus has a bus number in Arabic on the front in the driverʼs  upper area. 
It costs around 5 LE.
Microbuses: Microbuses are well known for their high speed. They are also very 
widespread in Cairo and all other governorates. 
They are smaller than buses. The fare diﬀers from one place to another depending on the 
distance and there is no ticket. 
Microbuses are easily accessed as they can be found at certain stations for 2 to 10 LE.
Taxis:  Taxis are one of the most preferable means of transportation in Egypt because they 
are faster and more relaxed. 
However, they are more expensive than other means. It is better to ask a local beforehand 
to avoid overpaying and kindly ask the driver to operate the meter or agree with him on the 
fare beforehand.

Travel Abroad Regulations

period, below are the regulations set by E-JUST;
• These regulations apply to all PG international students who receive scholarships.
• Student must submit a traveling abroad request to student aﬀairs oﬀice and the academic

department council approval, school council approval and education council approval ) 
and submit a copy to the international aﬀairs oﬀice and campus management.

dates).
