1.1. Highlighting the E-JUST Research-Oriented Identity 
As explicitly stated on E-JUST website, the university is identified as a research university 
with an ambition to cultivate an academic environment and become a benchmark for the Egyptian 
and African countries in education. This identity stems from the university’s pre-set Vision and 
Mission given below: 
 Vision 
 A status of national and international recognition. 
 A first-class international academic institution known worldwide for the high 
standard of its educational system, the high standard of its graduates and for the 
achievements of its research centers. 
 A world class center of excellence for higher education and research including 
regional and global reach. 
 One of the top 500 international universities within 10 years. 
 A success story and living proof of the Egyptian and Japanese cooperation that 
promotes human development in the region and the world. 
 Mission 
 To become a role model for graduate education and research institutions in Egypt 
by fostering the Japanese educational standards, policies, and systems. In this 
regard, E-JUST will foster links of collaboration between Egyptian and Japanese 
academic institutions. 
 To award academic degrees to E-JUST’s special graduates that hold a high status 
of international recognition and accreditation from local and international 
accrediting bodies. 
 To contribute to the enhancement and improvement of human resources in the 
region, by providing a high-level educational system and offering pragmatic and 
innovative solutions to human needs. 
 To promote and support the establishment of strong businesses, and strengthen 
technical and commercial ties between Japanese industries and organizations and 
their counterparts in countries and regions served by E-JUST.

for joining E-JUST.

any MSc and PhD thesis for holding the student’s defense and degrees award. 
 Offering funds for lab consumables of PG students. 
 Offering innovation and entrepreneurship training sessions and camps to UG and PG 
students.

2. Strategic Plan Executive Summary 
 
The Strategic Plan (SP) under consideration has been compiled by adopting a top-down 
approach in order to maximize the rightfully anticipated E-JUST role in supporting Egypt Vision 
2030 Goals, UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) and Africa Agenda 2063. Since 
nowadays many international and regional attributes do affect national education ecosystems, 
issues such the Fourth Industrial Revolution (4th IR) and different worldwide demographic 
expectations had to be taken into consideration. It should be stated that while composing this SP, 
pertinent and crucial data has been utilized and extracted from a considerable number of recently 
published international organizations relevant reports. Examples of these reports include United 
Nations Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization (UNESCO), United Nations Industrial 
Development Organization (UNIDO), International Labor Organization (ILO), Organization for 
Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD), World Bank (WB), World Economic Forum 
(WEF), and McKinsey & Partners.  
An executive summary of the SP, expressed in a single table form, is given in the following 
page. In this table, the correlations between the Strategic Plan Goals and the different 
implementation Strategic Actions are highlighted. In addition, the estimated timeline for the 
implementation of every Strategic Action/Sub-Action is also given. Details of the SP Goals and 
Stratgic Actions may be found in the relevant Sections of the SP 2026-2030 document.

3. Assessment of the International Future Job Market Needs, 
Demographic Expectations and Egypt Vision 2030 Considerations 
 
There is no doubt that drafting an effective E-JUST Strategic Roadmap should take into 
consideration a set of national, regional, and international aspects. These aspects are undoubtedly 
inter-tangled as a result of the current fast-changing technology-driven era. More specifically, the 
most important international aspects to be considered are:  
 International future job market needs mandated by the fourth industrial revolution (4Th IR) 
 Demographic future expectations 
 United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (UN SDGs) 
While these aspects seem to be incoherent, they do intersect from the human capacity 
building perspective and/or implications for which higher education (HE) institutions play some 
role.  
3.1. International future job market needs mandated by the fourth industrial revolution 
The fourth industrial revolution may be simply characterized by “disruptive smart 
automation.” This fact is clearly highlighted by the expected change in work hours nature as well 
as the human-machine job share as shown in Figs. 3.1-3.2. As can be seen from these two figures, 
more stress will be focused on technological and higher cognitive skills rather than physical and 
basic cognitive ones. However, social and emotional skills will remain in demand as such 
competencies are not easy to automate in the meantime. Obviously, human job opportunities are 
lost to machines as demonstrated by Fig. 3.2. As an expected consequence, perceived skill 
importance of today might not maintain the same importance in the near future. For instance, while 
basic data input and advanced data analysis are currently perceived in the high and low importance 
categories, respectively, this perception is expected to flip soon (please refer to Fig. 3.3).

jobs are expected to be generated during the period 2016-2030.  Needless to say, such job 
opportunities would only be available to university graduates that possess the right timely 
qualifications. In fact, a considerable responsibility rests on universities for properly adapting their 
curricula to fulfill the expected market needs. This is especially important since those needs may 
not be generally fulfilled by secondary school level education or less as demonstrated for the case 
of USA shown in Fig. 3.8.

3.2. Demographic future expectations 
While drafting a higher education strategy, employability goals should be set straight. 
Targeting national job markets while ignoring international employability prospects might lead to 
the wrong design of curricula as well as negative economic consequences. A bold index that must 
be taken into consideration is the future expectations of the ageing index, which represents the 
percentage ratio of population aged 65 and above to those aged below 15 as given by the following 
expression:  
𝐴𝑔𝑒𝑖𝑛𝑔 𝐼𝑛𝑑𝑒𝑥 (%) = 𝑃𝑜𝑝𝑢𝑙𝑎𝑡𝑖𝑜𝑛 ≥65 𝑦𝑒𝑎𝑟𝑠 𝑜𝑓 𝑎𝑔𝑒
𝑃𝑜𝑝𝑢𝑙𝑎𝑡𝑖𝑜𝑛 ≤15 𝑦𝑒𝑎𝑟𝑠 𝑜𝑓 𝑎𝑔𝑒× 100 
Hence, such index gives a futuristic expectation of retirees to those about to enter the work 
force. Figures 3.9 demonstrate the expected ageing index values for the European (EU) nations as 
a whole as well as for Japan. It should be pointed here that cases of EU have been specifically 
considered due to its proximity and historic ties to Egypt. Japan case consideration, on the other 
hand, is quite obvious for a study oriented to E-JUST specifics.

It can be inferred from Fig. 3.9 (a) that the shortage in EU workforce is rising constantly. 
More specifically, by 2035 it is expected that for every two retirees in EU about one EU graduate 
will be joining the work force. In countries where job creation is a challenge and where more

nations.

3.7, should be translated to the initiation and/or expansion of relevant programs in higher education 
institutions. Examples of these programs include ageing medication, public health, physical 
therapy, and biomedical engineering.

3.4.2. Demographic future expectations 
As mentioned in Section 3.2, the ageing index may give a futuristic expectation of retirees 
to those about to join the work force. As previously mentioned, this index has to be taken into 
consideration when it comes to projecting future employment prospects and consequent actions 
needed to be taken with regards to HE programs a-priori. Unlike for the case of the EU and Japan, 
where a future demand for workforce is indicated by their corresponding ageing index expectations 
given in Figs. 3.9, the situation is totally different for Egypt and other African nations. Figs. 3.23. 
gives the projected evolution of the ageing index values for Egypt in particular, the African 
continent in general, and North Africa-Middle East respectively. Fig. 3.23. (a) suggests that by the 
year 2035, three young people in Egypt will be joining the work force for every single retiree. 
Coupling this fact with EG Vision 2030 goals that plan to make university education available for 
a total of four million students by 2030, the magnitude of the job creation challenge in Egypt 
becomes evident. This challenge may indeed be partially alleviated by capitalizing on expected 
work force demand in the EU and other nations like Japan through the ramification of higher 
education programs addressing international job requirements. This fact will be later stressed in 
the detailed specifics of the E-JUST strategic planning activities and details. 
It is worth mentioning that E-JUST has taken several measures to qualify its graduates to 
the Japanese job market needs. More specifically, those measures include the following: 
 Most of E-JUST UG and PG curricula have been developed in collaboration with JCU 
partners. Moreover, most PhD students have a Japanese supervisor and get exposed to 
the Japanese technologies by spending few months in the Japanese advisor’s institute

4. Top-Down Collimation of E-JUST Strategic Plan Main Goals 
In Sections 3 a detailed and factual account of international job needs, UN SDGs, national 
job needs, demographic aspects as well as E-JUST Vision and Mission statement goals have been 
presented. Moreover, relevance to regional needs, especially for African nations, has also been 
highlighted whenever applicable. Logical and factual deduction of E-JUST strategic priorities may 
be inferred via a top-down methodology by identifying its potential role as an esteemed Egyptian 
HE institution in view of a categorized summary of all relevant international indicators as well as 
national and regional indicators and aspirations. It should be stressed here that some of these 
indicators and aspirations are either directly or indirectly inter-related. Such indicators and 
aspiration are: 
Goal #1. Support boosting the knowledge-based-economy value added in national industrial 
sectors and maximization of the GDP/capita through excellence in academia and innovation 
o Among the most important EG Vision 2030 Goals is to boost the economy and GDP/capita 
in general and to increase the value-added in national industries in particular. Obviously, 
this is the main recipe supported by statistics related to nations that dominate the world 
manufactured value-added and world manufactured exports. Responsibility resting on HE 
institutes in Egypt, as well as African nations, is extremely high due to the fact that, unlike 
in countries like Japan and Germany, the majority of FTE researchers are affiliated to 
universities. Hence, this can only be achieved via outsourced R&D activities in universities 
equipped with the right infrastructure and industry-oriented researchers. In addition, this 
also necessitates making available graduates capable of supporting R&D activities. 
o Boosting ICT-based service industries across all existing Egyptian industry and business 
sectors. This especially applies to transport, retail, tourism, and e-commerce sectors. Once 
more, having the relevant workforce expertise necessitate embedding a minimum level of 
ICT qualifications across all HE programs, including those falling within social sciences 
and humanities sectors.  
Goal #2. Increasing the intake of UG and PG international students 
In line with its role as a regional model research-oriented university, E-JUST is focusing on 
increasing the number of international undergraduate and postgraduate students, especially 
from Africa and the MENA region. This is goal intersects with a number of other goals 
especially increasing the number of African students within the TICAD and AJ-INSPIRE JICA 
co-funded programs as well as securing financial sustainability via targeting potential students 
from the Gulf Arab Region.   
Goal #3. Contribute to the supply of decent and productive jobs 
Availability of decent, productive and well-paid jobs is among the priorities of EG Vision 2030 
Goals. In case of the availability of job opportunities, having the matching qualifications 
among graduates becomes a must. As previously mentioned, ageing index figures indicate that 
this is a valid challenge for Egypt, African nations and many middle eastern countries. Based 
upon the international and national variables, fulfilling employability related EG Vision 2030 
Goals may be best achieved via the following three tracks: 
o Acquisition of internationally available job openings is a valid quest in view of the 
complementing nature of ageing index values in Egypt, and African nations, on one hand 
and other nations such as EU countries and Japan on the other hand. In other words, demand

employability venue for Egypt’s HE graduates provided that they are well-equipped with 
relevant education especially that covering aspects of the 4th Industrial Revolution. It is 
worth mentioning here that foreign currency remittances of Egyptians employed abroad 
represent a major percentage of the total national foreign currency influx. This percentage 
was 19.2% in the first quarter of the fiscal year 2020-2021 according to the Egyptian 
Ministry of Planning. This shows the importance of quality education to support the 
acquisition of international job openings by Egyptian HE graduates. Obviously, the same 
applies to African students studying in fine Egyptian HE institutes. This may be achieved 
by ramifying programs to be in line with the wide spectrum of the current, as well as future, 
national job needs in addition to necessary skills required to boost the value-added as

o Targeting nationally available and future job openings may be achieved by mapping the

supporting the economic and development aspirations of Egypt. 
o Augmenting HE programs with self-employability competencies is indeed another venue 
to address the challenge of job creation efforts. A country having a 100+ Million population 
has the potential to be a market accommodating new products and services. The same is 
absolutely true for African nations and other middle eastern countries. Augmenting all HE 
programs with relevant self-employability basic competencies such as entrepreneurship, 
project management, and marketing could open new opportunities to HE graduates who 
prefer to initiate their own business. In addition, having some basic 4th Industrial

interaction internet apps could even offer cross-border and export sales opportunities. Thus, 
further contributing to the national influx of foreign currency.  
Goal #4. Support to UN SDGs 
Support to UN SDGs would automatically be achieved in case the above-mentioned aspirations 
are materialized. The key factor is the well-being of citizens and this is usually accomplished 
through the high average salary/capita and the increased government spending on social 
support sectors including health and education. This is possible by attaining a high GDP/capita 
which may well be realized as a result of many of the above listed points such as boosting the 
value-added in national industries. It should be mentioned here that the Government of Egypt 
has been increasingly active in fulfilling UN SDGs and monitoring links between the 
fulfillment of EG Vision 2030 and supporting UN SDGs. As previously mentioned, the UN 
SDGs are usually, more or less, implicitly embedded in strategic plans of governments working 
on achieving economic prosperity and improving its citizens’ quality of life.

Egypt–Japan University of Science and Technology (E-JUST) is entering a pivotal new chapter: 
welcoming international undergraduate students from 2026/27. This is more than enrollment growth — it

East and Africa. 
At a time when families and governments are rethinking where to send their students abroad, E-JUST offers

 
Japanese academic rigor, delivered through JICA governance and 12 Japanese partner



Employability and outcomes, through industry-linked internships, research opportunities, and an

E-JUST will position itself as the “Japan-Inspired, Africa-Connected” university — a STEM hub that 
delivers rigorous education, nurtures innovation, and contributes to national development goals in Gulf and 
African markets. Internationalization is not only about attracting students; it is about advancing Egypt 
Vision 2030 as a regional education hub and extending Japan’s TICAD9 commitments to Africa.

 
Diversity: Ensure no single country exceeds 30% of the intake, with at least 35% female

 
Partnerships: Build pipelines through recognition agreements, scholarships, and industry 
sponsorships with ministries, high schools, and corporates.

Egypt Vision 2030: E-JUST’s targeted, high-quality internationalization contributes to the national goal of 
hosting 250,000 international students, endorsed by Egypt’s Prime Minister as a flagship outcome of 
TICAD9. 
Japan–Egypt Partnership: E-JUST is uniquely positioned as the only government-to-government 
university in the region, with JICA-backed labs, Japanese-trained faculty, and industry pathways. This 
makes it both a symbol of Japan’s soft power and Egypt’s education leadership.

2. Context and Rationale 
 
2.1. Global Trends in International Higher Education 
The global market for international education continues to expand despite geopolitical and economic 
pressures: 
 
Sustained Growth in Student Mobility: More than 6 million students studied abroad in 2023 
(UNESCO), a number projected to rise to 8 million by 2030. Families are increasingly looking 
beyond traditional destinations (US, UK, Canada) due to cost and visa constraints. 
 
Emergence of Affordable Hubs: Countries such as Malaysia, the UAE, and now Egypt are 
gaining ground by offering recognized, lower-cost, English-medium education in safe 
environments. 
 
High Demand for STEAM Disciplines: Over half of mobile students pursue science, technology, 
engineering, arts, or mathematics. E-JUST’s core academic offering matches this demand exactly. 
 
Changing Family Expectations: Today’s students and parents seek three guarantees: 
1. Return on Investment (clear career outcomes), 
2. Efficient Admissions (digital, fast, and transparent), 
3. Safe and Inclusive Environments (particularly important for Gulf families). 
 
Implication for E-JUST: With rising demand for affordable, high-quality STEAM education, E-JUST 
is well-placed to position itself as a credible alternative to expensive Western universities.

10. Risk Management & Mitigation 
No international marketing strategy is free of risks. What matters is not whether risks exist, but how well-
prepared E-JUST is to address them. The following framework identifies the most critical risk categories 
and outlines the mitigation steps that ensure continuity, credibility, and growth. 
 
10.1. 
Geopolitical & Economic Risks 
 
Nature of the Risk 
 
Regional instability: Political tensions in the Middle East or Africa may reduce student mobility. 
 
Currency fluctuations: Families in the Gulf and Africa are sensitive to tuition costs, which can be 
affected by exchange rates. 
 
Policy shifts: Sudden changes in visa policies, recognition processes, or education subsidies can 
disrupt recruitment. 
 
Global shocks: Events such as pandemics or conflicts can restrict travel and shift demand to online 
competitors. 
 
Mitigation Strategies 
 
Diversify market mix: Balance recruitment between Gulf (higher-paying, recognition-driven) and 
Africa (scholarship-driven, scale) so that downturns in one region do not cripple enrollment. 
 
Transparent cost planning: Publish tuition in USD (and EGP equivalents) with clear payment 
plans. Offer installment options and collaborate with local banks/fintech for flexible payments. 
 
Government alignment: Maintain close coordination with Egyptian, Japanese, and host-country 
ministries to anticipate policy changes early. 
 
Digital readiness: Expand virtual recruitment (webinars, WhatsApp counseling, virtual fairs) so 
outreach continues even if travel is restricted. 
 
Contingency planning: Set aside a 10–15% marketing contingency budget annually for crisis 
communication and emergency recruitment pivots. 
 
By spreading risk across regions, pricing smartly, and building digital resilience, E-JUST reduces 
vulnerability to shocks it cannot control.
